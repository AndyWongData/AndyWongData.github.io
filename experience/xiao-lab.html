Work Experience

Xiao Lab

Inorganic Chemistry Analyst

July 2019 — June 2022

Overview

At the Xiao Lab, I worked as a chemistry researcher studying metal-organic frameworks and other inorganic materials. My work involved synthesizing materials, collecting structural and compositional data, and using those measurements to understand whether an experiment had produced the result we expected.

Although I originally entered the lab as a chemistry researcher, the experience eventually changed the direction of my career. I realized that what interested me most was not necessarily the chemistry itself, but the information hidden in the data: the patterns between measurements, the clues that explained why an experiment succeeded or failed, and the story that emerged when all of those pieces were brought together.

What I Do

A large part of my work was turning experimental measurements into something I could reason about. I visualized structural and compositional data to look for relationships and patterns that could guide the development of new materials.

When an experiment did not produce the expected result, I used the collected data to investigate what had gone wrong. Rather than simply repeating the synthesis, I looked at the chemical composition and other measurements for evidence of the underlying problem, then used those findings to determine how the experiment should be adjusted or recovered.

That process was one of the first times I realized how much I enjoyed working with data. The measurements themselves were only individual pieces, but together they could explain what happened during an experiment and suggest what to do next.

I also learned how important it is to communicate that story. I reported findings to my Principal Investigator and mentor weekly, presented updates to the entire lab bi-monthly, and eventually shared my research with broader audiences through yearly presentations.

Impact

By using structural and compositional data to guide the research process, I contributed to the successful development of novel metal-organic framework analogues.

When analyzing chemical composition revealed synthetic errors, I was able to determine appropriate recovery responses that increased experimental yield by 50%.

The experience also gave me a foundation in communicating technical findings to different audiences, from discussing results directly with my research mentors to presenting work to the broader laboratory and general audiences.

The Beginning of the Pivot

Chemistry was where I first learned to ask questions with data. I eventually realized that it was the data—and the stories I could uncover from it—that I was most excited to follow. That realization is what ultimately led me from experimental chemistry toward data science.

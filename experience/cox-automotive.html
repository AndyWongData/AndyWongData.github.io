Work Experience

Cox Automotive

Data Science Intern

June 2023 — January 2024

01 — Overview

At Cox Automotive, I worked on using data science to improve used vehicle pricing recommendations. My work focused on understanding the relationships between vehicle attributes, market data, and list prices, then using those relationships to build and evaluate a more reliable pricing forecast.

02 — What I do

This internship gave me experience working through the full modeling process, from exploring a large dataset to deciding what information was actually useful for prediction.

I spent a lot of time investigating the data before modeling: looking at relationships between variables, identifying potential issues with the data, and testing different ways of representing the information. From there, I built and evaluated regression models and used the results to understand where the forecasts could be improved.

I also worked heavily with the underlying data. That meant writing SQL to bring together large datasets, thinking carefully about how the data should be cleaned, and using visualization to guide decisions about what to test next.

An important part of the experience was learning how to communicate technical work beyond the immediate project. I presented my findings through an intern showcase and bi-weekly company demonstrations, turning the work into something a broader audience could understand.

03 — Tools & Technologies

Python statsmodels SQL Snowflake Linear Regression Feature Engineering Data Visualization

04 — Impact

I built a linear regression model using statsmodels in Python to forecast used vehicle list prices and help improve the reliability of pricing recommendations.

Through feature engineering and the creation of interaction terms, I improved the accuracy of the pricing forecast model by 20%.

The project also gave me experience working with datasets containing more than 5 million rows, developing a stronger understanding of how data quality, feature selection, and exploratory analysis can affect the performance of a model.
